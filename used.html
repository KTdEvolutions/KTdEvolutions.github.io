Buying a Motorcycle

Whether you are buying new or new-to-you, take your time and know what you are buying. The excitement of finding the right motorcycle can make it easy to focus on the bike while overlooking the rest of the purchase. Price, condition, paperwork, insurance, maintenance, and additional ownership costs should all be considered before you bring it home.

Do your research before you shop and compare more than the advertised price. Ask questions, understand what is included in the purchase, and leave enough room in your budget for the things you will need after the motorcycle becomes yours. A good deal is only a good deal when the motorcycle and the purchase make sense for you.

Buying Your 1st New-to-Me Motorcycle

Your first motorcycle does not have to be new. A used motorcycle can give a new rider a less expensive way to start riding while leaving room in the budget for protective gear, training, maintenance, and other ownership costs.

The goal is not simply to find the cheapest motorcycle available. Look for a motorcycle that fits your needs, has been reasonably cared for, and gives you the opportunity to spend your time learning to ride instead of immediately repairing someone else's problems.

Before You Go See the Bike

Start by researching the motorcycle before contacting the seller. Compare the asking price with similar motorcycles and learn about the model you are considering. Knowing the motorcycle's basic specifications, common maintenance needs, and original equipment can make unusual details easier to recognize when you inspect it.

Read the entire listing and look closely at the photographs. Pay attention to the seller's description of maintenance, modifications, ownership history, and condition. If important information is missing, make a note of what you want to ask before arranging to see the bike.

Do Your Homework

  • Research the year, make, and model
  • Compare the asking price with similar motorcycles
  • Review the photographs carefully
  • Look at the motorcycle's original equipment and specifications
  • Write down questions before contacting the seller

Inspect the Motorcycle

Take your time when looking at a used motorcycle. Start with its overall condition and then look more closely at individual components. Tires, chain and sprockets, brakes, controls, fluids, suspension, and bodywork can all provide clues about how the motorcycle has been maintained.

Look for leaks, damaged fasteners, mismatched bodywork, unusual wear, and evidence of repairs. Modifications are not automatically a problem, but you should understand what has been changed and whether the work appears to have been completed carefully.

What to Check

  • Tires and overall tire condition
  • Chain and sprocket condition
  • Brake condition
  • Fluid levels and visible leaks
  • Forks and suspension
  • Lights, signals, switches, and controls
  • Bodywork and signs of previous damage
  • Aftermarket parts and modifications

Ask the Seller

A used motorcycle inspection is also a conversation with the seller. Ask about maintenance history, how long the motorcycle has been owned, why it is being sold, and whether it has been damaged or repaired.

Maintenance records and receipts can help support the motorcycle's history. You should also make sure the motorcycle's identifying information and ownership paperwork are consistent before agreeing to purchase it.

Questions to Ask

  • How long have you owned the motorcycle?
  • Why are you selling it?
  • Has the motorcycle ever been damaged or repaired?
  • What maintenance has been performed?
  • Do you have maintenance records or receipts?
  • What parts have been modified or replaced?
  • Are there any problems that currently need attention?

Know When to Walk Away

Finding a motorcycle you like can make it tempting to overlook problems. Try to evaluate the motorcycle based on its actual condition rather than how badly you want that particular listing to work out.

There will be other motorcycles for sale. If the condition, history, paperwork, or seller leaves you uncomfortable with the purchase, walking away is still a successful inspection.

Look Beyond the Basics

Experience changes what you may want from a used motorcycle and what you notice during an inspection. A rider shopping for another motorcycle may be evaluating upgraded suspension, aftermarket components, performance modifications, electronics, or evidence of harder use in addition to the basic condition of the bike.

The more specialized the motorcycle becomes, the more important it is to understand what you are looking at. A modification that adds value for one rider may add expense or uncertainty for another.
