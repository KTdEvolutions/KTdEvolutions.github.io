Gear & Safety

A motorcycle may be the most exciting part of getting into riding, but the motorcycle is only part of what a rider needs. Protective riding gear should be part of the plan and part of the budget from the beginning.

Motorcycle gear is designed for different types of riding, weather, protection, and comfort. Understanding the purpose of each piece can help you choose gear that you will actually wear every time you ride.

Gear for Your 1st Ride

Before spending the entire budget on a motorcycle, leave room for the equipment you will need to ride it. A helmet, gloves, protective clothing, and riding footwear should be considered part of getting started rather than purchases to worry about later.

Your first set of gear does not have to be the most expensive equipment available. Start with gear that fits correctly, provides appropriate protection, and works for the type of riding you plan to do.

Protect Your Head

Helmet

A motorcycle helmet is one of the most important pieces of equipment a rider will choose. Helmets are available in several styles and price ranges, but proper fit and appropriate safety certification should matter more than appearance alone.

Try on a helmet before deciding that a particular size or model is right for you. Different helmet shapes can fit differently even when the size printed on the label is the same.

Protect Your Hands

Gloves

Motorcycle gloves provide protection while still allowing the rider to operate the throttle, brake, clutch, and switches. They should fit securely without making it difficult to use the motorcycle's controls.

Different gloves are designed for different conditions. Short street gloves, full-gauntlet gloves, waterproof gloves, and insulated gloves may all make sense depending on how and where you ride.

Protect Your Body

Jackets & Pants

Motorcycle jackets and pants are designed to provide more protection than ordinary clothing. Riding equipment may use abrasion-resistant materials, protective armor, or a combination of both.

Leather and textile equipment each have advantages, and many riders eventually own more than one set of gear for different weather or riding situations. For a first set, focus on protection, fit, comfort, and whether you will realistically wear it.

Protect Your Feet

Riding Boots

Motorcycle-specific footwear can provide protection for the feet and ankles while still allowing the rider to operate the shifter and rear brake. Riding boots range from casual-looking street footwear to tall boots designed for sport, touring, or off-road riding.

As with the rest of your equipment, choose footwear that matches the kind of riding you actually plan to do.

Fit Matters

Protective equipment can only do its job properly when it fits the rider. Gear that is too loose may move out of position, while gear that is too tight can become uncomfortable or interfere with movement.

Try gear on in a riding position when possible. Something that feels comfortable while standing in a store may feel very different once you are sitting on a motorcycle.

YOUR nth RIDE

Build Gear Around Your Riding

As your riding changes, your equipment may change with it. Commuting, touring, sport riding, track riding, and off-road riding can place different demands on protective equipment.

Experience can also make it easier to recognize what you value in your gear. Ventilation, waterproofing, armor, abrasion resistance, visibility, communication systems, and convenience may become more important as you spend more time riding.
