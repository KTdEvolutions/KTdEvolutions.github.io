Ownership & Maintenance

Buying the motorcycle is the beginning of ownership, not the end of the purchase. Tires, fluids, brakes, chains, service intervals, and other maintenance items are all part of keeping a motorcycle ready to ride.

You do not have to become a motorcycle mechanic to be a responsible owner. Learning what your motorcycle needs, when it needs it, and what you can inspect yourself can help you recognize problems before they become bigger ones.

Everyth Ride Routine

Start by becoming familiar with your motorcycle and its owner's manual. The manufacturer provides maintenance schedules and information specific to the motorcycle you own.

Before you ride, the Motorcycle Safety Foundation recommends a pre-ride inspection using T-CLOCS. The acronym provides an easy way to remember the major areas of the motorcycle that should be checked.

T-CLOCS

  • T - Tires & Wheels
  • C - Controls
  • L - Lights & Electrics
  • O - Oil & Other Fluids
  • C - Chassis
  • S - Stands
View the MSF T-CLOCS Pre-Ride Inspection Checklist

Tires

Your tires are the motorcycle's connection to the road. Tire pressure, tread, wear, age, and visible damage should all be considered when evaluating their condition.

Different motorcycles and tires may have different recommendations, so use the specifications provided for your motorcycle rather than assuming that one pressure or replacement schedule applies to every bike.

Chain & Drivetrain

On a chain-driven motorcycle, the chain and sprockets require regular attention. Chain condition, adjustment, and lubrication can affect how smoothly power reaches the rear wheel.

Not every motorcycle uses a chain. Belt-driven and shaft-driven motorcycles have their own maintenance requirements, making the owner's manual an important reference regardless of what motorcycle you ride.

Oil & Fluids

Engine oil and other fluids have important jobs throughout a motorcycle. The fluids used, how they are checked, and when they are serviced depend on the motorcycle.

Follow the manufacturer's specifications and maintenance schedule rather than choosing service intervals based only on what another motorcycle requires.

Brakes

Brakes should be inspected as part of regular motorcycle ownership. Pay attention to changes in how the brakes feel and to the condition of components that can be visually inspected.

If something about the braking system does not look or feel right, determine the cause before continuing to ride. Work that is beyond your knowledge or experience can be handled by a qualified motorcycle technician.

Know Your Owner's Manual

Internet advice can be useful, but it does not replace the information written specifically for your motorcycle. Your owner's manual provides specifications, inspection information, maintenance intervals, and other information from the manufacturer. Today's bikes are becoming increasingly technology-laden and it's best not to fumble with controls when we should be focused on the road.

As you become more familiar with the motorcycle, the maintenance schedule can also help you plan for future service instead of being surprised when a larger service interval arrives. If it lives as long as we hope we do, a major service will follow us to the ends of time.

Set Up the Motorcycle for You

More experience can change the way you think about motorcycle setup. Instead of changing parts simply because aftermarket options exist, think about what you want the motorcycle to do differently and whether a modification actually helps accomplish that goal.

Suspension & Setup

Suspension settings can affect how a motorcycle feels and responds. As your riding develops, learning more about suspension and motorcycle setup can help you better understand the feedback the motorcycle gives you.

Tires for Your Riding

The right tire depends on how the motorcycle is actually being used. Commuting, touring, sport riding, track riding, and off-road riding can place very different demands on a tire.

Modify With a Purpose

Aftermarket parts can change appearance, comfort, protection, weight, sound, or performance. Before changing something, understand what problem you are trying to solve and what tradeoffs the modification may introduce.
